I don't often google the name of an essay I'm about to write but I did this time and, in doing so, came across another [_The Joy of Puttering_](https://whimsies.alexandermullan.com/p/the-joy-of-puttering). It was approximately what I was going to say about physical puttering. Close enough that I'll just leave it there. The irony that the hero image is AI-generated is not lost on me, but there's a good chance Mr. Alexander Mullan is himself a robot so let's all forgive robotic images amongst potentially robotic prose.

Anyway. Alexander's note is about puttering in a cabin and I also had this thought while puttering in my cabin. (Well, not _my_ cabin... "my" cabin is actually owned by a hacker collective nonprofit. But I'll continue to call it "my" cabin since that, for one reason or another, [pisses off strangers](https://youtu.be/26ys1p6VVs0?si=-msScH3UAyMLgfj8&t=2723) who assume I'm a rich Crypto Bro, based on no information whatsoever.) Anyway. While floating about the cabin, sweeping the floor, rearranging the bookcase, and absent-mindedly replumbing the shower, I realized that _puttering_ is absolutely my favourite activity. I don't believe puttering needs to be quite so aimless as Alexander describes it but it's certainly gentle.

This is precisely what Ankur Sethi is pointing to when he says he's [no longer using coding assistants on personal projects](https://ankursethi.com/blog/coding-assistants-personal-projects/). LLMs don't inherently write bad code, though they're certainly capable of doing that. They aren't inherently slower. They aren't inherently a bad idea.

LLMs are joyless.

I could have replumbed the shower as a chore. It could have been stressful and annoying and a box I needed to tick. It was none of those things. I took my time, enjoyed mostly-kinda-not-knowing-what-the-hell-I-was-doing, and leaned into a flawed process while I experimented, learned, and iterated. That's what programming has been for most of us, for a long time.

My friend Håkan Råberg, with whom I built [Endatabas](https://www.endatabas.com), has not yet tried LLMs. They were in their infancy when we shut down Endb and he's been working on a Masters in film since then. The last time he and I spoke on the phone, I was intrigued by how many of his guesses about LLM usage were correct: they're better at reviewing than writing code, left to their own devices they cause the codebase to drift, they don't "understand" evolutionary architecture, and they are joyless. When he came to this last point, he dug in further and asked a question, seemingly directed at no one, "I suppose we don't know, right? What is programming?"

What is programming. It seems a little obtuse on the surface but every response to executives flooding their engineering teams with token quotas touches upon the subject. _"Programming was never about typing!"_ they cried in response, for that first year after frontier models began accomplishing something. _"The robots will never have taste, you know?"_
